# Part 1 – Foundation

## 1. Every piece of data in this HRMS flows toward one output. What is that output, and why does everything else revolve around it?

The final output of the HRMS is accurate payroll for every worker.

All the information collected during the employee lifecycle—onboarding, attendance, overtime, pay structures, and deductions—flows into payroll. The purpose of the system is to ensure that every worker receives the correct salary on time.

If any step in the process contains incorrect data, the final salary payment will also be incorrect.

## 2. Who is the most vulnerable person in this system, and what does a system failure mean for them specifically — not in the abstract, but this month?

The most vulnerable person is the daily wage construction worker.

Most workers depend on their salary to buy food, pay rent, travel, and take care of their family. If attendance, overtime, or salary is calculated wrongly, they may get less money or receive their salary late.

Managers and HR may have extra work, but the worker and their family face the biggest problem because they depend on that salary.


## 3. Trace what happens when a site manager enters the wrong overtime hours for a worker. Where does that error travel, who should catch it, and who actually catches it?

If a site manager enters the wrong overtime hours, the wrong information goes into the payroll process.

The overtime is used to calculate the salary, create the payslip, and make the payment. If no one finds the mistake, the worker may get the wrong salary.

The system should stop wrong overtime entries. HR should check the overtime records before salary is processed. The payroll team should also check if there are any big changes in the salary.

This is why QA should focus on making sure the salary is correct, not just checking the screens.
